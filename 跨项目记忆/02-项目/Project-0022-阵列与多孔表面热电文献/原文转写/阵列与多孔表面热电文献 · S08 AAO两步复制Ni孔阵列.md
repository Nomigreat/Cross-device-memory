By Takashi Yanagishita, Kazuyuki Nishio,

Nanohole array structures with uniformly shaped and sized

holes and nanometer-scale dimensions have attracted growing

interest due to their potential applications in various types of

functional nanodevices.

[1 3] Anodicporousalumina,whichisa

typical self-organized nanohole array material formed by the

anodization of Al in an acidic solution, is promising for use

as a starting structure in nanofabrication.

[4,5] To expand the

applicability of anodic porous alumina, we have developed a

replication process for preparing metal or semiconductor

nanohole arrays using anodic porous alumina as a tem-

plate.

[6,7] In such a process, the preparation of a negative of

anodic porous alumina and the subsequent formation of the

positive generates metal or semiconductor hole arrays that

have geometrical structures identical to anodic porous alumi-

na. However, in the previously reported process, it is difficult

to form nanohole arrays with sufficiently straight holes when

the aspect ratios of the holes (depth divided by diameter) be-

come high. Polymer nanopillars in the negative of the array

cannot be maintained upright at high aspect ratios because

the nanopillars are supported only by a layer at the base. In

Such nanopillar arrays can be used as the negative for nano-

metal nanohole array of Ni was prepared based on a process

broadens the scope for the potential application of nanohole

arrays from ordered anodic porous alumina.

Figure 1 shows a schematic for the preparation of Ni nano-

hole arrays with high aspect ratios. The procedure consists of

two main processes; preparation of negative nanopillar arrays

of poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA), and the subsequent

formation of nanohole arrays of a metal. The details are de-

scribed in the Experimental section.

Figure2 shows a typical scanning electron microscopy

(SEM) image of the negative-type PMMA prepared from

anodic porous alumina. From the SEM image, it can be seen

that nanopillars composed of PMMAwere formed at uniform

intervals. Both ends of each pillar are supported by polymer

supporting layers, and thus all the pillars stand upright, paral-

lel to one another. It was confirmed that the sizes and shapes

of the pillars were uniform. The diameters and lengths of the

nanopillars were 300 nm and 2.5 lm, respectively, which were

in accordance with the dimensions of the anodic porous alu-

mina used as a template. In Figure 2, partial constrictions

were observed in the nanopillars. These constrictions were

caused by mechanical deformation of the pillars during frac-

turing of the samples for SEM observation.

Figure 3 shows a surface SEM image of the Ni hole array

membrane, obtained by dissolving the negative-type PMMA

after the electrochemical deposition of Ni. From the SEM im-

and interval were formed in the sample. The hole size and in-

the dimensions of the anodic porous alumina template. The

Ni hole array membrane shown in Figure 3 was 1 mm wide

and had a channel depth of 0.1 mm, corresponding to the di-

mensions of the PMMA negative shown in Figure 1. The Ni

deposition time for the sample shown in Figure 3 was 30 h.

The high-magnification SEM images of both sides of the Ni

nanohole array shown in Figure 4 demonstrate the retention

of the ordered hole arrangement throughout the replication

process. The consistency of the hole arrangement from one

surface of the Ni membrane to the other verifies the perfect

retention of the hole arrangement in the process.

The cross-sectional SEM image of the Ni hole array shown

in Figure 5 also shows the retention of the straight-hole-array

feature after replication. In Figure 5, all the holes are con-

firmed to be straight and parallel to each other. The thickness

of the obtained metal hole array membrane formed by the

process is expected to correspond to that of the porous alumi-

na membrane used as a template. The thickness of the Ni

membrane in Figure 5 was measured to be 6 lm (aspect ratio

of 20), corresponding to the thickness of the alumina tem-

plate.

In conclusion, Ni nanohole arrays with straight holes of high

aspect ratio were fabricated using a new replication process.

The present process enables the precise replication of anodic

porous alumina with high aspect ratios and will be used for

the preparation of ordered nanohole arrays of various types

of materials. In addition, the unique nanopillar array struc-

tures used as a negative in the present process can be used for

various types of nanodevices, e.g., photonic crystals, due to

their upright structure and high aspect ratios.

Experimental

The highly ordered anodic porous alumina used as a template was

obtained by a combination of pre-texturing by imprinting followed by

anodization of Al [8]. An ordered array of shallow concave structures

was prepared on Al (99.99% purity) by imprinting using a SiC mold

with a hexagonally patterned array of convex structures. The pre-tex-

tured Al was anodized at a constant voltage of 200 V in 0.1 M phos-

phoric acid at 0C for 10 min. The hole size was adjusted by post-

etching in 10 wt.

-% phosphoric acid at 30C for 60 min. After the

post-etching treatment, the Al substrate was dissolved in a saturated

HgCl2 solution. The bottom part of the oxide film was removed using

an Ar ion-milling apparatus at a beam voltage of 6 kV for 45 min.

An anodic porous alumina membrane with through-holes was

dipped in a methyl methacrylate (MMA) monomer with 5 wt.

-% ben-

zoyl peroxide as an initiator for polymerization. After polymerization

by UV irradiation, the sample was polished to expose the two sides of

theembeddedanodicporousaluminamembrane.AthinPt Pdlayer

was coated onto the surface of the sample by sputtering, and then a

Au reinforcing layer, which acted as an electrode for the metal deposi-

tion of hole array materials, was formed electrochemically. Au elec-

trodeposition was carried out in a plating solution (NE-CHEMCAT,

ECF-60)ataconstantcurrentof5mAcm 2

. After the formation of

the Au electrode, the alumina membrane was dissolved in 10 wt.

-%
